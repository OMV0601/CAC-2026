# Lexicon · Demo Video Script (final)

**Target length:** about 2:35 (the limit is 3:00)
**Speakers:** Om (7 parts) and Soham (6 parts)

## How to record

- Record **one MP3 per part**, named **exactly** like the headings below (for example `01-om.mp3`).
- A phone voice memo app is fine. Use a quiet room, with the phone about a hand's width from your mouth.
- Leave about **1 second of silence** at the start and end of each file. I trim it.
- Talk like you're explaining it to a friend: calm and clear, a little slower than normal.
- Messed up? Just re-record that one file.
- Say "Lexicon" the same way every time: **LEK-si-con**.

## Rules checklist

| Rule | Where |

| Every team member's name | Parts 03 and 04 (you each say your own name), plus on screen |

## 01-om.mp3 · The hook
*On screen: Sam rocking, then pieces of the story pop up as you say them: "12 years old", "Rocks + Low hum = Stomach pain", "Mom: away", "2:00 AM", and the nurse's wrong guess.*

> This is Sam. He's twelve, and he can't speak.
> When his stomach hurts, he rocks and hums, low. His mom knows that.
> But this weekend she's away, and Sam ends up in the ER at 2 AM.
> The nurse sees him rocking, and thinks he's just anxious.

## 02-soham.mp3 · Why not just call his mom?
*On screen: "Just call his mom?" and four short reasons: Not there 24/7, Confidently wrong, A sound won't fit in a call, Calls get forgotten.*

> Why not just call his mom?
> Because most hours of Sam's life, she isn't in the room.
> And the nurse doesn't even know she's wrong, so she'd never call.

*On screen: both our photos. A spotlight and an arrow land on Om during "I'm Om".*

> I'm Om,

## 04-soham.mp3 · Who we are (part 2) and what Lexicon is
*On screen: the spotlight moves to Soham, then the Lexicon title, three steps (films, scans, found in seconds), and who it's for.*

> and I'm Soham.
> Meet Lexicon: a video dictionary of a non-speaking person's signals that any nurse or aide can look up in seconds.
> It's built for families of people who can't speak, and for the strangers who care for them.

## 05-om.mp3 · Demo: the family side
*On screen: mom's phone. She records a clip, labels it, and makes a QR code.*

> Before she leaves, Sam's mom films the things only she understands, and says what each one means.
> Rocking with a low hum means pain, usually his stomach, so she tags it as a pain sign.
> Then she makes a QR code for his weekend that only works for three days.

## 06-om.mp3 · Demo: the nurse looks it up
*On screen: the nurse's phone. Scan, the grid of clips, tap, tap, side by side, match.*

> The nurse scans it. No account, no app.
> Every clip plays at once, so she can just watch.
> She taps where it's happening, and that it's a sound. Six clips become one.
> She films three seconds and compares it side by side with mom's clip. Same thing.
> It's pain, and now she knows exactly what to do.

## 07-soham.mp3 · Demo: asking the family (the big moment)
*On screen: both phones. The nurse sends a clip, mom's phone buzzes, and the answer appears.*

> But what if it's something mom never recorded?
> The nurse films five seconds and taps send.
> Mom's phone buzzes, wherever she is. She types what it means…
> and the answer appears on the nurse's screen, without her touching anything.
> Mom can save it, so the next nurse never has to ask.

## 08-soham.mp3 · Demo: trust
*On screen: the honest "we don't recognise this" answer, the code turning off, the "Who looked" list.*

> If nobody recognizes something, Lexicon says so, instead of guessing.
> Mom can turn a code off anytime, and it stops working instantly.
> And she can see everyone who looked.

## 09-om.mp3 · Our one rule
*On screen: two cards. "Interpret" (an AI guess) gets an X, "Retrieve" (mom's own words) gets a check.*

> That's our one rule. Lexicon never interprets. It only retrieves.
> No AI decides what a movement means, because a wrong answer about pain is worse than no answer.

## 10-soham.mp3 · How we built it
*On screen: the animated architecture diagram.*

> We built Lexicon in TypeScript and SQL, with React and Tailwind, hosted on Vercel.
> Supabase gives us the database, video storage, and the live connection that delivers mom's answer.
> Security rules we wrote in SQL guard every request, and fifty-six automated tests try to break them.
> Phone alerts use Web Push.

## 11-om.mp3 · What we learned
*On screen: "The code" vs. "Lexicon won't…" with Guess, Diagnose, Interpret, Pretend to know crossed out.*

> The hardest part wasn't the code. It was deciding what Lexicon should refuse to do.

## 12-soham.mp3 · Ending, part 1
*On screen: mom as a dashed outline, "not in the room".*

> Sam's mom can't always be in the room.

## 13-om.mp3 · Ending, part 2
*On screen: a phone playing mom's clip, then the end card with your names, the website and a QR code.*

> Now, her voice can be. This is Lexicon.
